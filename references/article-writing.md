# Writing the article

The article is for someone who hasn't watched the video and won't. After reading it they should know what was said, why it matters, and where to be skeptical.

## Shape

1. **Lede (1 paragraph)** — who talked to whom, about what, and the one-sentence answer to "so what?". Conclusion first; don't make the reader wait.
2. **Body sections (5–8 `h2`)** — follow the video's logic, not its minute-by-minute order. One idea per section; its heading states the idea ("信任的第二层：它会不会不择手段" beats "第二部分").
3. **Reading notes / critical reading (last section)** — 3–4 short points: what is genuinely new, what the speaker sidestepped, which claims lack evidence, what to watch next. Fair, specific, no cheap shots.
4. **source_note** — title, channel, length, that the text is based on the transcript, and which numbers are unverified claims.

Length scales with substance, not minutes: roughly 1,500–2,500 Chinese characters (900–1,500 English words) for a 15–30 minute video, 3,000–4,500 (1,800–2,500) for about an hour, and at most ~6,000 (3,500) for two hours or more — a long conversation needs tighter selection, not a longer article.

## Craft

- **Argument chain**: each paragraph should follow from the previous one. If a section is a list of facts, find the claim that ties them together and lead with it.
- **Concrete over abstract**: keep the speaker's own examples (the doctor appointment, the DMV website, the museum of ice). They are what readers remember.
- **Attribute**: "Wang 认为…", "主持人指出…", "据视频引用的新闻…". Never present a speaker's claim as established fact, and never add numbers, dates or names the video doesn't give.
- **Paraphrase**: short direct quotes only where the exact wording matters (≤ 1 sentence, a few per article).
- **Diagrams and tables**: when the speaker lays out a framework, a sequence, a timeline or a comparison, draw it as a `diagram` or `table` block. Aim for 2–4 per article; they matter most when the video itself has no graphics.
- **Figures**: put a figure right after the paragraph it illustrates, with a caption that tells the reader what to notice ("越往右越依赖事前审批"), not just a label. Two related frames can sit side by side in one figure block. 4–7 figures in the article; the rest live in the gallery.
- **Plain language**: short sentences, no filler phrases, explain jargon once in passing.

## Mind map vs article vs cards

They overlap on purpose but do different jobs: the mind map is the whole video at a glance (coverage), the article is the argument (understanding), the cards are the facts and frameworks worth keeping (memory). Don't paste the same sentences into all three.
